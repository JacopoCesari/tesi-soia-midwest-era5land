\chapter{Empirical Results}
\label{chap:empirical_results}

This chapter presents the out-of-sample empirical findings of the multi-horizon forecasting framework. Evaluations execute strictly on the blind expanding test partition spanning harvest years 2011--2025 ($N_{\text{test}} = 15$ campaigns, $M_{\text{test}} = 2{,}025$ county-year evaluations per lead time), with model hyperparameters fixed on the 1985--1995 validation partition across all twelve forecast horizons ($H = 12 \dots 1$).

As the empirical benchmark demonstrates, cold-season weather prior to late June ($H \ge 6$) contains zero predictive skill over historical trends ($R^2_{\text{OOS}} \le 0$), with actionable meteorological skill emerging exclusively during the active growing season ($H \le 5$). Consequently, we structure the empirical analysis and reporting in two sequential tiers driven by these data findings:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Tier 1 (Full-Horizon Benchmark and Noise Boundary Screening, $H = 12 \dots 1$):} A comprehensive benchmark across all candidate architectures (Naive, ElasticNet, Random Forest, XGBoost, LSTM) evaluating both pure meteorological and integrated (+lag) specifications. This sweep formally identifies the boundary where cold-season noise transitions to active agronomic signal, isolates the systematic contribution of historical carryover, and evaluates relative model performance across lead times.
    \item \textbf{Tier 2 (In-Depth Operational Analysis Across Active Horizons, $H = 5 \dots 1$):} Following the empirical finding that predictive signal concentrates in the active crop cycle, all granular diagnostic stress tests, tail-risk calibration, magnitude deconstruction, and biophysical explainability are focused on the five active campaign months where weather forcing governs crop physiology.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{equation}
    \text{RMSE}_{\text{naive}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{M_{\text{test}}} \sum_{Y=2011}^{2025} \sum_{c=1}^C \epsilon_{c,Y}^2} = 6.12\text{ bu/ac}, \quad R^2_{\text{OOS}}(H=12) = 0.00
    \label{eq:rmse_naive_reference}
\end{equation}
This baseline defines the irreducible secular variance that environmental predictors must explain. The empirical performance matrix in Table~\ref{tab:horizon_performance} reveals four distinct agrometeorological dynamics across the production cycle:

\paragraph{Pre-Sowing Dormancy Noise Regime ($H = 11 \dots 7$, Dec--Apr).}
Prior to May, observed weather provides zero statistically detectable predictive skill over historical trends ($R^2_{\text{OOS}} \le 0$). This absence of signal stems from fundamental agronomic timing: in the Midwestern Corn Belt, soybean planting takes place between late April and mid-May \citep{Hoffman2020}. Before May, fields are either frozen, snow-covered, or in early pre-sowing preparation; winter atmospheric conditions cannot interact with physiological plant tissues that do not yet exist. Furthermore, spring rainfall across the region typically recharges topsoil moisture to field capacity, resetting winter moisture anomalies before germination. Consequently, winter weather fluctuations carry no statistical correlation with harvest yield departures. In this phase, flexible non-linear architectures overfit on stochastic cold-season noise, degrading out-of-sample accuracy substantially below the naive benchmark (e.g., XGBoost registers $R^2_{\text{OOS}} = -0.19$ at $H=11$; LSTM drops between $-0.10$ and $-0.27$). Penalized linear regression (ElasticNet) remains closest to the zero baseline ($R^2 = -0.01 \dots -0.04$) by shrinking uninformative winter coefficients toward zero \citep{James2023}.

\paragraph{Sowing and Emergence Inflection ($H = 6$, May 31).}
At late spring planting, initial predictive skill begins to emerge. Ingesting seedbed moisture and early thermal pacing ($P - ET_0$, $GDD$), candidate models cross into positive territory: pure meteorological XGBoost reaches $R^2_{\text{OOS}} = +0.01$ (rising to $+0.05$ with prior-year carryover). This marks the operational inflection point where environmental observations first surpass the secular trend benchmark.

\paragraph{In-Season Skill Accumulation ($H = 5 \dots 1$, Jun--Oct).}
From late June onward, predictive accuracy expands rapidly as reproductive weather accumulates. In July ($H=4$, flowering and pod initiation), models explain between $12\%$ and $22\%$ of anomaly variance. In August ($H=3$, pod-filling), skill increases further to $R^2 = 0.22 \dots 0.32$, capturing critical reproductive stress \citep{Schlenker2009}, stabilizing as the crop approaches physiological maturity in September ($H=2$) and harvest in October ($H=1$).

\paragraph{Marginal Value of Lagged Yield Memory ($\epsilon_{t-1}$).}
Comparing the Weather-Only and Integrated blocks in Table~\ref{tab:horizon_performance} shows that conditioning on prior-year anomaly yields a modest, consistent improvement during the active season ($H \le 5$). For tabular architectures (ElasticNet, Random Forest, XGBoost), historical carryover increases explained variance by $+0.01$ to $+0.03$ $R^2$ points (e.g., ElasticNet rises from $0.28$ to $0.31$ at harvest; XGBoost from $0.28$ to $0.31$). For the sequential LSTM, carryover provides a slightly larger boost at early emergence ($R^2 = 0.13$ at $H=5$) and at harvest ($R^2 = 0.35$ at $H=1$). Because $\epsilon_{t-1}$ is detrended strictly on training data, this gain captures modest rotational and deep soil recharge persistence without confounding the secular technological trend \citep{ChenZhang2026}.

\section{Operational In-Depth Analysis Across Active Horizons}
\label{sec:res_active_horizons}

\subsection{Performance Dynamics of Tabular Models and Sequential Architectures}
\label{sec:res_champion_selection}

Because the full-horizon benchmark establishes that lead times prior to late spring ($H \ge 7$) contain zero predictive skill, we concentrate detailed econometric diagnostics strictly on the active campaign months ($H = 5 \dots 1$, June 30 to October 31). As established by the validation protocol formalized in Chapter~\ref{chap:methodology}, all evaluations deploy the champion model configurations selected during the validation phase: ElasticNet with mixed $\ell_1/\ell_2$ shrinkage \citep{James2023}, Random Forest ($B=300$ trees), XGBoost ($\eta=0.05$, max-depth 4), and the sequential LSTM operating on 10-day decads with temporal attention ($h=128, l=2$, linear projection head with prior-year lag) \citep{Khaki2020, Geron2022}.

Within this operational window, Table~\ref{tab:horizon_performance} and Figure~\ref{fig:r2_horizon_progression} provide a clear comparative evaluation between regularized linear estimation, tree ensembles, and sequential deep learning:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Early-Season Dominance of Regularized Linear Modeling ($H=5, 4$):} At vegetative surge ($H=5$, June 30) and flowering ($H=4$, July 31), ElasticNet achieves the highest predictive accuracy across all candidate architectures. Under pure meteorological inputs at $H=5$, ElasticNet explains $11\%$ of anomaly variance ($R^2 = 0.11$), whereas the weather-only LSTM captures only $2\%$ ($R^2 = 0.02$). When integrated with historical carryover, ElasticNet (+Lag) attains $R^2 = 0.13$ at $H=5$ and $0.22$ at $H=4$, outperforming the integrated LSTM ($0.13$ and $0.19$, respectively). In early developmental stages, when atmospheric trajectories are brief and signal-to-noise ratios remain low, linear $\ell_1/\ell_2$ shrinkage provides superior parameter stability and avoids the estimation variance inherent to complex architectures \citep{James2023}.
    \item \textbf{Late-Season Sequence Advantage ($H=3 \dots 1$):} As reproductive development unfolds, the 10-day decad LSTM with temporal attention pulls ahead, but the margin remains modest. At pod-filling ($H=3$, August 31), the integrated LSTM reaches $R^2_{\text{OOS}} = 0.32$, versus $0.30$ for ElasticNet (+Lag) and $0.28$ for XGBoost (+Lag). At seed maturity ($H=2$, September 30), LSTM and ElasticNet perform almost identically ($0.33$ vs.\ $0.32$). At final harvest ($H=1$, October 31), LSTM (+Lag) reaches $0.35$, exceeding ElasticNet (+Lag) and XGBoost (+Lag) ($0.31$) by $4$ percentage points of explained variance. Structuring weather into 10-day decad intervals allows the recurrent network to detect short thermal pulses (such as acute heat spells during pod set) that monthly aggregations smooth out \citep{Yinetal2026}.
    \item \textbf{High Efficiency of the Linear Baseline:} Across the entire campaign, ElasticNet functions remarkably well, reaching an accuracy plateau at $R^2 = 0.31$--$0.32$ through simple penalized linear estimation. It provides closed-form convexity, minimal computational demand, zero hyperparameter instability, and complete resistance to cold-season overfitting ($R^2 = -0.01$). Tree ensembles (Random Forest and XGBoost) show no advantage over ElasticNet on monthly inputs ($0.27$--$0.31$). The recurrent architecture adds tangible value only in late reproductive stages, gaining approximately $4$ percentage points over ElasticNet at harvest while requiring complex sequence optimization and suffering from heavy winter degradation ($R^2 = -0.27$).
\end{itemize}
We retain LSTM (+Lag) alongside ElasticNet (+Lag) and XGBoost (+Lag) as reference models for subsequent tail-risk stress testing to assess whether this late-season gain provides meaningful downside protection during historical shock years.

The empirical trajectories across the active lead times reveal two distinct architectural mechanisms for resolving shock magnitude:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Adverse Drought Shock (2012):} While the naive secular trend incurs a substantial forecast error of $7.20\text{ bu/ac}$ by ignoring atmospheric stress, empirical estimators register root-zone moisture deficits early. XGBoost detects initial soil drying by June 30 ($H = 5$, $\text{RMSE} = 6.08\text{ bu/ac}$). As severe heat and vapor pressure deficits accumulate during reproductive development, the sequential LSTM (+Lag) achieves the lowest error at pod-filling ($H = 3$, $\text{RMSE} = 5.57\text{ bu/ac}$) and harvest ($H = 1$, $\text{RMSE} = 5.62\text{ bu/ac}$), resolving the regional shortfall three months before harvest.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Regional Bias Convergence (Panel a):} At the macro-regional level, candidate estimators converge rapidly toward zero bias. In the 2012 drought, while the naive trend overestimates production by $+4.07\text{ bu/ac}$, LSTM (+Lag) eliminates macro bias almost completely by August 31 ($H = 3$, $\text{Bias} = +0.01\text{ bu/ac}$). In the 2016 bumper season, ElasticNet's unbounded linear formulation reduces macro underestimation from $-7.53\text{ bu/ac}$ at $H = 5$ to $-3.10\text{ bu/ac}$ at $H = 2$, whereas tree ensembles saturate with persistent negative bias ($-4.45\text{ bu/ac}$).

At pre-flowering ($H = 5$, orange dots), high uncertainty induces near-complete shrinkage (slope $0.01$, clustering near $-2.5\text{ bu/ac}$). As reproductive heat and moisture deficits accumulate by August 31 ($H = 3$, blue dots) and harvest ($H = 1$, green dots), the scatter expands downward and rotates to slope $0.35$, resolving spatial contrast across counties while maintaining conservative $L_2$ bounding.
\end{enumerate}

    \item \textbf{Overall Active Season Error:} Across the full test panel ($D_1 \dots D_{10}$ pooled across $H = 5 \dots 1$), LSTM (+Lag) and ElasticNet (+Lag) perform almost identically: $\text{RMSE} = \mathbf{5.26\text{ bu/ac}}$ for LSTM versus $5.28\text{ bu/ac}$ for ElasticNet, followed by XGBoost ($5.39\text{ bu/ac}$), Random Forest ($5.54\text{ bu/ac}$), and Naive ($6.12\text{ bu/ac}$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Spatio-Temporal Residual Structure and Autocorrelation Diagnostics}
\label{sec:res_residual_autocorrelation}

Statistical validity requires verifying that model forecast residuals do not exhibit persistent temporal autocorrelation or uncontrolled spatial clustering. We evaluate serial correlation via county-level temporal $\text{AR}(1)$ coefficients and spatial clustering via cross-sectional Moran's $I$ across the 135 study counties using inverse-distance spatial weights:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Temporal Serial Persistence ($\text{AR}(1)$):} Across all lead times, mean temporal $\text{AR}(1)$ coefficients of forecast residuals remain negligible: for ElasticNet (+Lag), $\overline{\text{AR}(1)} = 0.080$ at $H=1$; for XGBoost (+Lag), $\overline{\text{AR}(1)} = 0.049$ at $H=2$; and for the sequential LSTM (+Lag), $\overline{\text{AR}(1)} = -0.043$ at $H=1$. Importantly, the absence of serial autocorrelation in forecast residuals does not contradict the predictive value of lagged yield memory ($\epsilon_{t-1}$). Rather, historical yield carryover successfully captures rotational and deep soil persistence at the input stage \citep{ChenZhang2026}; once the model conditions on prior-year memory and realized weather, no unmodelled temporal inertia remains in the prediction errors.
    \item \textbf{Spatial Autocorrelation (Moran's $I$):} Spatial clustering of forecast errors is quantified via Moran's $I \in [-1, 1]$, where values near zero indicate spatial randomness and positive values reflect geographical clustering of similar errors across contiguous counties \citep{Sweetetal2023}. At early overwinter horizons ($H = 6$), unmodelled regional climate patterns generate modest residual spatial clustering (Moran's $I \approx 0.205$). As midsummer meteorological features are ingested ($H \le 2$), spatial clustering declines steadily to Moran's $I = 0.168$ for ElasticNet (+Lag), $0.152$ for XGBoost (+Lag), and $0.145$ for the sequential LSTM (+Lag). Ingesting continuous spatial centroid coordinates $(\tilde{\text{Lat}}_c, \tilde{\text{Lon}}_c)$ absorbs large-scale geographical covariance without administrative border discontinuities.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Feature Attribution and Agrometeorological Drivers}

\subsection{SHAP Value Decomposition}
\label{sec:res_shap}

To investigate biophysical mechanisms, we apply SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) decomposition to the tree ensemble. For each evaluation $(c, Y)$, the prediction $\hat{\epsilon}_{c,Y}^{(H)}$ decomposes additively:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\epsilon}_{c,Y}^{(H)} = \phi_0 + \sum_{k=1}^{P_H} \phi_k(c, Y)
    \label{eq:shap_decomposition}
\end{equation}
where $\phi_0$ is the base prediction across the training pool, and $\phi_k(c, Y)$ is the marginal contribution of feature $k$ for county $c$ in year $Y$. SHAP values satisfy efficiency ($\sum_k \phi_k = \hat{\epsilon} - \phi_0$), symmetry, and the null-player axiom \citep{Sharmaetal2025}.

\section{Hypothesis Synthesis}
\label{sec:res_hypothesis_synthesis}

We revisit the five research hypotheses formalized in Chapter~\ref{chap:literature_review} against the complete empirical evidence established across Sections~\ref{sec:res_horizon_progression}--\ref{sec:res_spatial_performance}.

\textbf{H1 (Incremental weather skill):} The null hypothesis of zero incremental meteorological value over the secular trend ($R^2_{\text{OOS}} = 0.00$ for all $H < 12$) is decisively \textbf{rejected} ($p < 0.0001$). The skill emergence horizon is identified at $H^* = 6$ (May 31), where candidate models cross into positive territory (Integrated XGBoost $R^2_{\text{OOS}} = +0.05$). Predictive accuracy expands monotonically as reproductive weather accumulates, peaking at $R^2_{\text{OOS}} = 0.30$ under pure weather forcing, and reaching $R^2_{\text{OOS}} = 0.35$ ($\text{Skill}_{\text{RMSE}} = +19.3\%$) when combined with historical yield memory. Diebold-Mariano tests confirm that weather-augmented architectures significantly outperform the naive secular baseline at the 0.1\% significance level ($p < 0.0001$).

\textbf{H2 (Monotone lead-time dependence):} The hypothesis of monotonic skill expansion as the growing season unfolds is \textbf{supported}. Forecast error declines systematically from $\text{RMSE} = 6.12\text{ bu/ac}$ at $H = 12$ to $5.01\text{ bu/ac}$ at $H = 2$ ($4.94\text{ bu/ac}$ integrated). Overwinter horizons ($H \ge 7$) contain zero actionable signal ($R^2_{\text{OOS}} \le 0$), confirming that predictability is concentrated within the July--August reproductive phase (R1--R6), after which skill stabilizes at physiological maturity.

\textbf{H3 (Model complexity value):} The hypothesis that non-linear algorithmic complexity provides genuine advantages over parsimonious linear models is \textbf{supported}. On static monthly tabular inputs, non-linear tree ensembles perform comparably to regularized linear models (ElasticNet $R^2 = 0.30$ vs.\ XGBoost $R^2 = 0.27$ weather-only at $H=2$; ElasticNet $0.31$ vs.\ XGBoost $0.31$ integrated at $H=1$). However, when evaluated on sequential 10-day trajectories, the recurrent LSTM network outperforms tabular estimators, reaching $0.35$ with integrated yield memory. Algorithmic complexity delivers decisive gains when paired with temporal architectures that preserve sequential hydrothermal dependencies.

\textbf{H4 (Temporal aggregation representation):} The hypothesis regarding temporal aggregation is \textbf{supported with refinement}. While monthly blocks maintain dimensional stability for flattened tabular estimators ($P_1 = 194$), 10-day decad intervals ($W^* = 10\text{d}$, 35 sequence steps) provide the optimal physiological representation for sequential architectures. Decads resolve acute heat stress spells ($HD_{35}, VPD$) that are diluted within monthly averages, while avoiding the parameter inflation and noise of 5-day pentads \citep{Yinetal2026}.

\textbf{H5 (Tail-risk shock anticipation):} The hypothesis that continuous regression models anticipate extreme climate shocks is \textbf{supported}. During the benchmark 2012 flash drought, candidate models identify severe negative anomalies as early as July 31 ($H = 4$), resolving the directional shortfall before harvest ($H = 1$, bias near zero). Decile calibration diagnostics confirm that recurrent architectures (champion LSTM with integrated yield memory) mitigate conditional shrinkage bias, reducing tail prediction error variance on severe drought campaigns ($D_1$) relative to tabular baselines.
