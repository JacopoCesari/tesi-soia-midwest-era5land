\chapter{Empirical Results}
\label{chap:empirical_results}

This chapter presents the out-of-sample empirical findings of the multi-horizon forecasting framework. Evaluations execute strictly on the blind expanding test partition spanning harvest years 2011--2025 ($N_{\text{test}} = 15$ campaigns, $M_{\text{test}} = 2{,}025$ county-year evaluations per lead time), with model hyperparameters fixed on the 1985--1995 validation partition. Following the two-tier methodology established in Section~\ref{sec:meth_two_tier_strategy}, we structure the empirical narrative along a disciplined progression:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Tier 1 (Generalist Multi-Horizon Screening, $H = 12 \dots 1$):} A comprehensive benchmark across all candidate architectures (Naive, ElasticNet, Random Forest, XGBoost, LSTM) evaluating both pure meteorological and integrated (+lag) specifications. This sweep formally identifies the boundary between cold-season noise and active agronomic signal, isolates the systematic contribution of historical carryover, and establishes the overall champion architecture.
    \item \textbf{Tier 2 (In-Depth Operational Analysis, $H = 5 \dots 1$):} Concentrating diagnostic stress tests, tail-risk calibration, magnitude deconstruction, and biophysical explainability strictly on the five active campaign months where weather variables drive crop physiology.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Delineating the Cold-Season Noise Boundary \texorpdfstring{($H \ge 6$)}{(H >= 6)}}
\label{sec:res_noise_boundary}

The empirical matrix in Table~\ref{tab:horizon_performance} and the progression trajectories in Figure~\ref{fig:r2_horizon_progression} reveal a stark bifurcation across the twelve monthly horizons:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The Noise Regime ($H = 11 \dots 6$, Dec--May):} Overwinter weather (snow cover, soil freeze, dormant moisture) and early spring conditions provide zero statistically detectable predictive skill over historical trends ($R^2_{\text{OOS}} \le 0$). The deterministic secular trend remains the optimal forecast. In this dormant phase, increasing algorithmic complexity is actively detrimental: high-capacity architectures (deep tree ensembles and recurrent networks) overfit on stochastic cold-season fluctuations, degrading out-of-sample accuracy below the naive benchmark (e.g., XGBoost at $H=11$ registers $R^2_{\text{OOS}} = -0.187$; LSTM reaches $-0.101$ to $-0.268$). Penalized linear regression (ElasticNet) remains closest to the zero baseline ($R^2 = -0.014$ to $-0.039$) by shrinking uninformative winter coefficients toward zero.
    \item \textbf{Transition at Vegetative Emergence ($H = 6$, May 31):} At late spring planting, marginal skill begins to emerge. Ingesting seedbed moisture and thermal pacing ($P - ET_0$, $GDD$), XGBoost reaches $R^2_{\text{OOS}} = +0.007$ (rising to $+0.051$ when combined with prior-year soil carryover), marking the first operational inflection point where meteorological inputs beat the naive secular trend.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Agronomic Memory Ablation: Systematic Value of Lagged Carryover}
\label{sec:res_ablation_lag}

Comparing the left (Weather-Only) and right (Integrated) blocks of Table~\ref{tab:horizon_performance} evaluates the marginal value of multi-year agronomic memory ($\epsilon_{t-1}$) across all model families:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Tabular Consistency:} Across ElasticNet, Random Forest, and XGBoost, conditioning on prior-year anomaly yields a modest but strictly positive gain during the active season ($H \le 5$), expanding explained variance by $+0.005$ to $+0.033$ $R^2$ points (e.g., ElasticNet at harvest rises from $0.279$ to $0.306$; XGBoost from $0.277$ to $0.310$).
    \item \textbf{Sequential Deep Amplification:} For the 10-day decad LSTM, carryover integration delivers an exceptional structural boost. At early vegetative emergence ($H = 5$, June 30), where pure weather explains $1.7\%$ of variance, the integrated LSTM expands accuracy to $R^2_{\text{OOS}} = 0.126$ ($\text{Skill} = +6.5\%$, $\text{RMSE} = 5.73\text{ bu/ac}$), providing crucial early-season traction. At harvest resolution ($H = 1$), the integrated LSTM surges from $0.284$ to $\mathbf{0.349}$ ($\text{RMSE} = 4.94\text{ bu/ac}$, $\text{Skill} = +19.3\%$).
\end{itemize}
Crucially, because $\epsilon_{t-1}$ is detrended strictly on training data prior to ingestion, this $+6.5$ percentage-point gain represents genuine multi-season carryover (deep root-zone recharge and rotational resilience) rather than spurious technological drift leakage. Because conditioning on historical carryover improves skill across every model family without architectural distortion, we retain the integrated (+lag) specification as the standard configuration for all subsequent in-depth analyses.

\section{Operational In-Depth Analysis Across Active Horizons}
\label{sec:res_active_horizons}

\subsection{The Unconditional Champion Architecture: Sequential LSTM (+Lag)}
\label{sec:res_champion_selection}

Because the preliminary generalist sweep establishes that lead times prior to late June ($H \ge 6$) contain zero predictive skill, we concentrate all in-depth econometric investigations strictly on the five active campaign months ($H = 5, 4, 3, 2, 1$, June 30 to October 31).

Within this operational window, Table~\ref{tab:horizon_performance} resolves an essential methodological question: does the champion model vary by lead time, or does a single architecture dominate the entire growing season?
The empirical evidence is definitive:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Sequential Superiority:} The 10-day decad LSTM with temporal attention (+Lag) achieves the top overall performance across the decisive reproductive and harvest milestones: $R^2_{\text{OOS}} = 0.126$ at $H=5$, $0.315$ at $H=3$, $0.331$ at $H=2$, and $\mathbf{0.349}$ at $H=1$.
    \item \textbf{The Tabular Ceiling:} In contrast, tabular architectures (ElasticNet, Random Forest, XGBoost) plateau at $R^2_{\text{OOS}} \approx 0.31$--$0.32$ (ElasticNet $0.320$ at $H=2$, XGBoost $0.310$ at $H=1$). Monthly averages aggregate away short-duration thermal pulses: three consecutive days of $T_{\text{max}} \ge 35^\circ\text{C}$ during pod-set cause severe flower abortion, yet exert negligible influence on a 31-day mean. By structuring weather into 10-day decad steps with Bahdanau attention, the recurrent network preserves pulse sequencing, outperforming the best tabular baseline by $+3.9$ percentage points at harvest ($0.349$ vs.\ $0.310$, $p = 0.00015$).
\end{itemize}
Consequently, we select the **LSTM (+Lag)** architecture as the primary champion model for in-depth diagnostic stress testing, retaining **ElasticNet (+Lag)** and **XGBoost (+Lag)** as the top tabular reference baselines.

\subsection{Deconstructing Magnitude: Explained Variance versus Natural Crop Volatility}
\label{sec:res_magnitude_deconstruction}

A critical challenge in agricultural machine learning is interpreting the practical magnitude of predictive skill against real-world crop yield volatility. Commercial soybean yields in the Midwestern Corn Belt exhibit two distinct variance layers:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Secular Trend Dominance:} Over the 75-year panel, technological advancements (genetics, synthetic fertilizers, precision machinery) account for over 60\% of total raw yield variance ($\tau_{c,Y}$).
    \item \textbf{Residual Anomaly Dispersion:} The detrended climatic anomaly ($\epsilon_{c,Y}$) exhibits an unconditional standard deviation of $\sigma_{\epsilon} \approx 6.12\text{ bu/acre}$ across the 2011--2025 test window, corresponding to approximately $\pm 12\text{--}15\%$ of average harvest yields.
\end{enumerate}

In normal growing seasons, weather conditions hover within physiological comfort zones ($22\text{--}26^\circ\text{C}$, adequate rainfall). In these years, biological yield is dictated by genetic potential, and observed anomalies deviate by only $\pm 1\text{--}3\text{ bu/acre}$ around the trend line. In such benign conditions, meteorological predictors explain a modest fraction of variance, and forecast errors of $4.5\text{--}5.0\text{ bu/acre}$ have the same magnitude as the natural annual fluctuations.

This distribution exposes why reporting binary \textit{directional hit rates} (e.g., claiming a model predicts the sign of the anomaly 71\% of the time) is scientifically uninformative. When realized anomalies cluster tightly near zero, predicting a minor positive deviation ($\hat{\epsilon} = +0.2\text{ bu/ac}$) when actual yield is $+0.5\text{ bu/ac}$ counts as a nominal directional success, but offers zero risk management value. 

What matters decisively to the agricultural supply chain is capturing the \textbf{magnitude of tail deviations}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Can the model detect when an anomaly will exceed $-10\text{ bu/acre}$ in a severe drought?
    \item How does error magnitude ($\text{RMSE} = 4.94\text{ bu/ac}$) scale against the natural dispersion ($\sigma = 6.12\text{ bu/ac}$)?
\end{itemize}
The champion LSTM (+Lag) achieves a net error reduction of $1.18\text{ bu/acre}$ ($\text{Skill} = +19.3\%$). While a 1.2 bu/ac gain appears modest in normal years, its economic impact is massive during extreme tail regimes, where it prevents catastrophic supply misallocations.

\subsection{Spatio-Temporal Residual Structure and Autocorrelation Diagnostics}
\label{sec:res_residual_autocorrelation}

Statistical validity requires verifying that model residuals do not exhibit persistent temporal autocorrelation or uncontrolled spatial clustering. We evaluate serial correlation via county-level temporal $\text{AR}(1)$ coefficients and spatial clustering via cross-sectional Moran's $I$ across the 135 study counties using inverse-distance spatial weights:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Temporal Persistence ($\text{AR}(1)$):} Across all lead times, mean temporal $\text{AR}(1)$ coefficients remain negligible: for ElasticNet, $\overline{\text{AR}(1)} = 0.085$ at $H=2$ and $0.080$ at $H=1$; for XGBoost, $\overline{\text{AR}(1)} = 0.049$ at $H=2$; and for the 10-day LSTM PReLU model, $\overline{\text{AR}(1)} = -0.033$ at $H=2$ and $-0.043$ at $H=1$. These near-zero serial correlations confirm that train-only linear detrending removes historical trend inertia, preventing persistent multi-year residual bias.
    \item \textbf{Spatial Autocorrelation (Moran's $I$):} In early overwinter horizons ($H = 6$), unmodelled regional climate patterns generate modest residual spatial clustering (Moran's $I \approx 0.205$). As midsummer meteorological features are ingested ($H \le 2$), spatial clustering declines steadily to Moran's $I \approx 0.156$ for LSTM PReLU and $0.145$ for LSTM Linear with lag. Ingesting continuous spatial centroid coordinates $(\tilde{\text{Lat}}_c, \tilde{\text{Lon}}_c)$ absorbs large-scale geographical covariance without administrative border discontinuities.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Feature Attribution and Agrometeorological Drivers}
\label{sec:res_feature_attribution}

\subsection{SHAP Value Decomposition}
\label{sec:res_shap}

To investigate biophysical mechanisms, we apply SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) decomposition to the tree ensemble. For each evaluation $(c, Y)$, the prediction $\hat{\epsilon}_{c,Y}^{(H)}$ decomposes additively:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\epsilon}_{c,Y}^{(H)} = \phi_0 + \sum_{k=1}^{P_H} \phi_k(c, Y)
    \label{eq:shap_decomposition}
\end{equation}
where $\phi_0$ is the base prediction across the training pool, and $\phi_k(c, Y)$ is the marginal contribution of feature $k$ for county $c$ in year $Y$. SHAP values satisfy efficiency ($\sum_k \phi_k = \hat{\epsilon} - \phi_0$), symmetry, and the null-player axiom \citep{Sharmaetal2025}.

\subsection{Phenological Driver Mapping}
\label{sec:res_phenological_drivers}

\section{Hypothesis Synthesis}
\label{sec:res_hypothesis_synthesis}

We revisit the five research hypotheses formalized in Chapter~\ref{chap:literature_review} against the complete empirical evidence established across Sections~\ref{sec:res_horizon_progression}--\ref{sec:res_spatial_performance}.

\textbf{H1 (Incremental weather skill):} The null hypothesis of zero incremental meteorological value over the secular trend ($R^2_{\text{OOS}} = 0$ for all $H < 12$) is decisively \textbf{rejected} ($p < 0.0001$). The skill emergence horizon is identified at $H^* = 6$ (May 31), where candidate models cross into positive territory (Integrated XGBoost $R^2_{\text{OOS}} = +0.051$). Predictive accuracy expands monotonically as reproductive weather accumulates, peaking at $R^2_{\text{OOS}} = 0.297$ under pure weather forcing, and reaching $R^2_{\text{OOS}} = 0.349$ ($\text{Skill}_{\text{RMSE}} = +19.3\%$) when combined with historical yield memory. Diebold-Mariano tests confirm that weather-augmented architectures significantly outperform the naive secular baseline at the 0.1\% significance level ($p < 0.0001$).

\textbf{H2 (Monotone lead-time dependence):} The hypothesis of monotonic skill expansion as the growing season unfolds is \textbf{supported}. Forecast error declines systematically from $\text{RMSE} = 6.12\text{ bu/ac}$ at $H = 12$ to $5.01\text{ bu/ac}$ at $H = 2$ ($4.94\text{ bu/ac}$ integrated). Overwinter horizons ($H \ge 7$) contain zero actionable signal ($R^2_{\text{OOS}} \le 0$), confirming that predictability is concentrated within the July--August reproductive phase (R1--R6), after which skill stabilizes at physiological maturity.

\textbf{H3 (Model complexity value):} The hypothesis that non-linear algorithmic complexity provides genuine advantages over parsimonious linear models is \textbf{supported}. On static monthly tabular inputs, non-linear tree ensembles perform comparably to regularized linear models (ElasticNet $R^2 = 0.296$ vs.\ XGBoost $R^2 = 0.268$ weather-only at $H=2$; ElasticNet $0.306$ vs.\ XGBoost $0.310$ integrated at $H=1$). However, when evaluated on sequential 10-day trajectories, the recurrent LSTM network outperforms tabular estimators, reaching $0.349$ with integrated yield memory. Algorithmic complexity delivers decisive gains when paired with temporal architectures that preserve sequential hydrothermal dependencies.

\textbf{H4 (Temporal aggregation representation):} The hypothesis regarding temporal aggregation is \textbf{supported with refinement}. While monthly blocks maintain dimensional stability for flattened tabular estimators ($P_1 = 194$), 10-day decad intervals ($W^* = 10\text{d}$, 35 sequence steps) provide the optimal physiological representation for sequential architectures. Decads resolve acute heat stress spells ($HD_{35}, VPD$) that are diluted within monthly averages, while avoiding the parameter inflation and noise of 5-day pentads \citep{Yinetal2026}.

\textbf{H5 (Tail-risk shock anticipation):} The hypothesis that continuous regression models anticipate extreme climate shocks is \textbf{supported}. During the benchmark 2012 flash drought, candidate models identify severe negative anomalies as early as July 31 ($H = 4$), resolving the directional shortfall before harvest ($H = 1$, bias near zero). Decile calibration diagnostics confirm that recurrent architectures with PReLU projection heads mitigate conditional shrinkage bias, reducing tail prediction error variance on severe drought campaigns ($D_1$) relative to tabular baselines.
