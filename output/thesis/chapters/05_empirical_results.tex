\section{Lead-Time Dynamics and Progression across Horizons}
\label{sec:res_horizon_progression}

At $H=12$ (October 31 of the preceding year), forecasts predict a zero yield anomaly ($\hat{\epsilon}_{c,Y} = 0$) by construction, projecting solely the county-specific secular technological trend $\hat{\tau}_{c,Y}$. The out-of-sample root mean squared error of this naive benchmark defines the baseline error reference across the 2011--2025 test panel:
\begin{equation}
    \text{RMSE}_{\text{naive}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{M_{\text{test}}} \sum_{Y=2011}^{2025} \sum_{c=1}^C \epsilon_{c,Y}^2} = 6.12\text{ bu/ac}, \quad R^2_{\text{OOS}}(H=12) \equiv 0.00
    \label{eq:rmse_naive_reference}
\end{equation}
This baseline provides the normalizing denominator in Equation~\eqref{eq:r2_oos}. Positive $R^2_{\text{OOS}}$ values at subsequent horizons ($H = 11 \dots 1$) consequently quantify the incremental predictive skill contributed by observed agrometeorological conditions beyond the secular trend.

Table~\ref{tab:horizon_performance} compares candidate models across the twelve forecast horizons under two information sets: a pure meteorological feature space and an integrated specification incorporating the prior-year detrended yield anomaly ($\epsilon_{c,Y-1}$). At the final horizon ($H=1$), all specifications implement harvest closure $H^*(s)$, freezing the information set on September 30 for Minnesota (Maturity Group I) and October 31 for the remaining states to eliminate post-harvest atmospheric noise. Consistent with the validation tuning in Section~\ref{sec:meth_lstm}, the weather-only LSTM employs a PReLU projection head, while the integrated architecture uses a linear projection head.

\input{tables/tab_horizon_performance}

The empirical performance matrix in Table~\ref{tab:horizon_performance} and corresponding trajectories in Figure~\ref{fig:r2_horizon_progression} track predictive skill across the production cycle at the granular, disaggregated county level ($M_{\text{test}} = 2{,}025$ out-of-sample county-year evaluations). Evaluating individual counties requires models to absorb localized microclimates and residual variance without the error cancellation inherent in spatial aggregation. Across the twelve monthly horizons, performance follows three distinct chronological regimes:

Before May ($H = 11 \dots 7$, Dec--Apr), weather-only models do not improve upon the secular trend benchmark. These monthly forecast origins precede the primary planting window documented by \citet{Hoffman2020}. Integrated ElasticNet remains close to the baseline ($R^2_{\text{OOS}} \approx 0.00$), while flexible non-linear estimators generate negative scores, reflecting variance inflation and overfitting on uninformative antecedent noise.

With the inclusion of May planting-window observations ($H = 6$, May 31), initial predictive skill begins to emerge across candidate architectures: pure meteorological XGBoost crosses into positive territory at $R^2_{\text{OOS}} = +0.01$ (rising to $+0.05$ with prior-year carryover). This marks the operational inflection point where environmental observations first surpass the secular trend benchmark.

Predictive accuracy accelerates as reproductive weather accumulates across summer ($H = 5 \dots 1$, Jun--Oct). Substantial performance gains concentrate during flowering (July, $H=4$) and pod filling (August, $H=3$), when moisture deficits and thermal stress directly dictate seed mass. Across all candidate architectures, skill reaches a plateau proximate to physiological maturity, with marginal increments between September and October.

Incorporating prior-year detrended yield memory ($\epsilon_{t-1}$) provides a consistent, incremental improvement of approximately $+0.03$ points in $R^2_{\text{OOS}}$ across all model families at harvest closure (ElasticNet rises from $0.29$ to $0.32$, LSTM from $0.34$ to $0.37$, and the ensemble from $0.35$ to $0.38$). In contrast to previous studies that report massive variance gains from autoregressive lags on raw yields \citep{ChenZhang2026}, our controlled detrending isolates genuine multi-year agronomic memory---such as deep soil moisture carryover and crop rotational inertia---from secular technological drift.

\section{Comparative Model Performance across Active Horizons}
\label{sec:res_active_horizons}

Across the active growing season ($H = 5 \dots 1$, June--October), out-of-sample forecast accuracy reveals three core empirical insights on model complexity and inductive biases:

First, penalized linear regression (ElasticNet) achieves the highest individual accuracy from early vegetative growth through seed filling ($H = 5 \dots 2$, June to September), reaching $R^2_{\text{OOS}} = 0.32$ (with lag). This performance underscores the statistical value of regularized parsimony in high-dimensional climate panels \citep{James2023}: when the physical signal is partially accumulated, shrinking uninformative coefficients heavily restricts estimation variance. Non-linear tree ensembles (Random Forest and XGBoost) fail to surpass ElasticNet on monthly tabular features, generating lower scores ($R^2_{\text{OOS}} \le 0.30$) due to terminal-leaf variance under localized sample sizes.

Second, the recurrent neural network (LSTM) progressively accelerates skill as reproductive weather unfolds, surpassing ElasticNet at harvest closure ($H=1$, reaching $R^2_{\text{OOS}} = 0.37$ with lag, and $0.34$ weather-only). While monthly tabular models collapse intra-monthly heat spikes into 30-day averages, the LSTM's 10-day decad representation preserves the duration and compound sequencing of acute temperature and moisture stress during critical pod-filling stages \citep{Yinetal2026}. Parameter sharing across recurrent cells provides structural regularization against variance inflation without expanding the parameter count.

Third, the equal-weighted convex ensemble (50\% ElasticNet + 50\% LSTM) consistently achieves champion performance across all active horizons, culminating in $R^2_{\text{OOS}} = 0.38$ at harvest closure (and $0.35$ under weather-only forcing). This synergy stems from the empirical orthogonality of model forecast residuals ($r < 0.70$): linear shrinkage excels during near-normal growing regimes by eliminating variance, while recurrent temporal processing better captures non-linear damage thresholds during acute stress periods.

Having established the systematic empirical gain of prior-year detrended yield carryover, all subsequent stress tests, macro-regional evaluations, and diagnostic attributions focus on the integrated model specifications. For notational brevity, these models are hereafter designated simply as ElasticNet, Random Forest, XGBoost, LSTM, and Ensemble.

\section{Inferential Significance and Tail-Risk Stress Testing}
\label{sec:res_significance_tail_risk}

\subsection{Pairwise Predictive Superiority: Diebold--Mariano Tests}
\label{sec:res_significance}

To establish whether predictive gains over naive secular trends and between competing model families are statistically distinguishable from sample variability, we evaluate one-sided Diebold--Mariano tests on annual mean squared loss differentials across the 15 out-of-sample test years (2011--2025). Aggregating loss differentials to annual cross-sections preserves the contemporaneous spatial covariance structure across the 135 counties within each crop year. Heteroskedasticity and Autocorrelation Consistent (HAC) inference is estimated with two annual lags and the small-sample $15/14$ covariance correction.

At the June emergence horizon ($H=5$), weather-only ElasticNet and integrated LSTM achieve statistically significant loss reductions over the naive trend benchmark, with nominal $p$-values of $0.0011$ and $0.00025$ ($\text{DM} = 3.06$ and $3.48$). Conversely, tree ensembles fail to reject the null hypothesis of equal accuracy at the $5\%$ level (Random Forest $p=0.074$; XGBoost $p=0.094$). From July ($H=4$) onward, all evaluated weather-informed models reject equal accuracy against the naive baseline ($p < 0.0001$).

At harvest closure ($H=1$), pairwise testing between LSTM and ElasticNet yields $\text{DM} = 0.99$ ($p = 0.162$). Despite a $+0.050$ point improvement in $R^2_{\text{OOS}}$ and a $0.188\text{ bu/ac}$ reduction in RMSE, the sequential deep architecture does not achieve statistically significant superiority over penalized linear shrinkage under serial correlation controls. The Linear-Recurrent Ensemble significantly improves upon ElasticNet at the $5\%$ level ($\text{DM} = 2.11$, $p = 0.017$), while its performance difference from LSTM remains statistically indistinguishable ($\text{DM} = 0.33$, $p = 0.371$). These tests confirm that while meteorological forcing provides unequivocal predictive skill, pooled point rankings between flexible neural networks and regularized linear baselines do not establish uniform statistical dominance.

\section{Agrometeorological Drivers and Attribution Across Scales}
\label{sec:res_feature_attribution}

\subsection{Attribution Methodology and Surrogate Recurrent Mapping}
\label{sec:res_shap_attribution}

We evaluate agrometeorological sensitivities across model paradigms using Grouped Permutation Feature Importance \citep[GPFI;][]{Sweetetal2023} across 19 predictor groups: 17 weather indicators, lagged yield, and jointly permuted spatial coordinates on the 2011--2025 out-of-sample test panel. For each physical indicator $j$, all constituent monthly features across the full campaign ($m \in \{\text{Nov}, \dots, \text{Oct}\}$) are permuted jointly across test instances ($\pi_j$). Grouping across all campaign months preserves the seasonal correlation profile while breaking the association with realized yield anomalies. Predictive degradation is quantified by out-of-sample explained variance loss:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta R^2_{\text{OOS}}(j) = \max\left(0, \, R^2_{\text{OOS}}(\text{base}) - R^2_{\text{OOS}}(\pi_j)\right)
    \label{eq:pfi_metric}
\end{equation}
where the non-negativity constraint neutralizes sampling noise from uninformative features.

Because baseline predictive accuracy differs across candidate architectures, absolute variance losses are normalized into a Relative Information Share (\%):
\begin{equation}
    s_j = \frac{\Delta R^2_{\text{OOS}}(j)}{\sum_{k=1}^{19} \Delta R^2_{\text{OOS}}(k)} \times 100\%
    \label{eq:pfi_share}
\end{equation}
Normalizing into relative information shares allows direct comparison of attentional distributions across model families.

For the sequential LSTM, permuting input channels across three-dimensional sequence tensors ($N \times 36 \times 19$) severely disrupts recurrent hidden state dynamics and entails prohibitive computational cost. We therefore implement a Global Surrogate Model \citep{Sweetetal2023}: a gradient-boosted tree ensemble (XGBoost) trained to replicate the predictions of the LSTM in anomaly space. The surrogate achieves near-perfect fidelity ($R^2 = 0.9968$ for $H=1$ and $R^2 = 0.9998$ for $H=2$, with $\text{RMSE} < 0.18\text{ bu/ac}$), legitimizing its use for post-hoc agrometeorological attribution.

Figure~\ref{fig:feature_attribution_benchmark} reports the Relative Information Share across all 19 predictor groups for ElasticNet, XGBoost, and the LSTM surrogate at harvest closure horizon $H^*(s)$, while Figure~\ref{fig:shap_horizon_stack} aggregates these individual shares into five coherent macro-agronomic domains.

Because atmospheric demand and hydrological supply variables are highly collinear, individual architectures select substitute proxies reflecting their inductive biases:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{XGBoost} operates as an acute thermal-stress sensor: it is dominated by Atmospheric Demand and Heat ($28.9\%$, driven by non-linear thresholds of $VPD$ at $18.9\%$) and Thermal Regimes ($34.6\%$), while assigning minimal weight to multi-year yield memory ($4.1\%$).
    \item \textbf{ElasticNet} distributes predictive weights across Thermal Regimes ($43.0\%$), Atmospheric Demand ($25.6\%$), and Soil Moisture ($20.9\%$), selecting dewpoint temperature ($T_{\text{dew}}$, $27.9\%$) as a parsimonious linear proxy for surface moisture.
    \item \textbf{LSTM} acts as a cumulative water balance integrator and memory anchor: predictive shares are led by Hydrological Supply ($29.7\%$, dominated by $P - ET_0$ at $24.9\%$) and Memory and Spatial Baseline ($25.1\%$, where lagged yield contributes $21.2\%$), capturing soil moisture recharge and multi-year agronomic persistence.
\end{itemize}

\section{Anomaly Space versus Raw Yield Performance}
\label{sec:res_raw_vs_anomaly}

We reconstruct physical yield forecasts by adding the training-only trend to the predicted anomaly, following Equation~\eqref{eq:yield_reconstruction}:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{y}_{c,Y}^{(H)} = \hat{\tau}_{c,Y} + \hat{\epsilon}_{c,Y}^{(H)}
    \label{eq:level_projection}
\end{equation}
As established in Section~\ref{sec:meth_loss_metrics}, RMSE and MAE are algebraically identical between anomaly and reconstructed yield spaces because trend extrapolation $\hat{\tau}_{c,Y}$ is constant across models. Table~\ref{tab:raw_vs_anomaly_performance} reports performance metrics across both spaces at harvest closure across the $2{,}025$ test evaluations.

\input{tables/tab_raw_vs_anomaly_performance}

Evaluating predictions on reconstructed physical yield levels ($\hat{y} = \hat{\tau} + \hat{\epsilon}$) clarifies the distinction between secular agronomic progress and interannual weather skill (Table~\ref{tab:raw_vs_anomaly_performance}). The naive secular trend accounts for $47.4\%$ of total raw yield variance ($r = 0.746$) across the test partition without ingesting meteorological data, capturing multidecadal genetic breeding, fertilizer efficiency, and permanent cross-county soil endowments.

Conditioning on ERA5-Land surface reanalysis and historical carryover raises explained variance to $66.8\%$ ($r = 0.833$) for LSTM and $67.3\%$ ($r = 0.838$) for the Linear-Recurrent Ensemble, resolving an incremental $+19.9$ percentage points of commercial yield variation beyond technological momentum.

This dual evaluation reconciles the apparent disconnect with agricultural machine learning studies that report $R^2 \ge 0.75$ on raw yield levels without prior detrending \citep{ChenZhang2026, Khaki2020}. Un-detrended estimators inadvertently attribute the deterministic technological upward trend to smooth climate predictors, generating spurious correlation and inflated apparent skill. Formulating the primary forecasting benchmark in detrended anomaly space isolates genuine biophysical predictability ($R^2_{\text{OOS}} = 0.378$), confirming that empirical models capture true climate-induced deviations from trend.

\section{Macro-Regional Portfolio Aggregation and Supply-Chain Value}
\label{sec:res_macro_portfolio}

While county-level estimation ($M_{\text{test}} = 2{,}025$) provides the econometric degrees of freedom required to calibrate non-linear stress thresholds without sample starvation, commercial supply-chain decision-makers operate on aggregate regional volumes. Table~\ref{tab:yearly_macro_yields} presents regional mean yield anomaly forecasts at harvest closure across the 15 test campaigns (2011--2025), ordered by realized anomaly severity.

\input{tables/tab_yearly_macro_yields}

When county forecasts are aggregated to the regional portfolio scale, idiosyncratic local error components cancel via the law of large numbers ($\sigma / \sqrt{N}$), generating a substantial surge in predictive accuracy:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Out-of-sample explained variance rises from $R^2_{\text{OOS}} = 0.378$ at county level to $R^2_{\text{macro}} = 0.730$ for the Linear-Recurrent Ensemble and $0.726$ for the LSTM.
    \item Forecast error magnitude contracts sharply: portfolio RMSE drops from $4.83\text{ bu/ac}$ at county level to $2.15\text{ bu/ac}$ for the ensemble (a $44\%$ error reduction relative to the naive macro baseline of $3.84\text{ bu/ac}$).
    \item Correlation with realized regional production anomalies reaches $r = 0.880$ for the ensemble and $0.910$ for the LSTM. After adding the secular trend to reconstruct absolute production levels, Pearson correlation reaches $r = 0.946$ (ensemble) and $0.954$ (LSTM).
\end{itemize}

While unobserved localized factors induce residual scatter at county level, macro-regional aggregation transforms empirical agrometeorological learning into a highly reliable forecasting engine for commercial risk management.
