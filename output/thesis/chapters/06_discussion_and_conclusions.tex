\chapter{Discussion and Conclusions}
\label{chap:discussion_conclusions}

This chapter synthesizes the empirical evidence, evaluates findings against the literature, formalizes verdicts on research hypotheses H1--H5, and presents operational implications, methodological recommendations, and future research trajectories.

\section{Synthesis of Research Questions and Empirical Findings}
\label{sec:disc_synthesis}

The empirical investigation resolves five central research questions regarding the predictability of county-level crop yields from surface weather:

\subsection{When Does Surface Weather Become Actionable?}
The twelve-horizon progression ($H=12 \dots 1$) establishes that surface weather reanalysis provides zero out-of-sample forecast skill during pre-sowing and early vegetative windows ($H=12 \dots 5$, November through May). Out-of-sample scores remain bounded near zero ($R^2_{\text{OOS}} \le 0.00$), demonstrating that antecedent winter and spring weather does not anticipate summer growing conditions. Informative predictive skill emerges distinctly during the reproductive pod-filling phase in July and August ($H=4 \dots 2$). By late August ($H=3$), model accuracy increases markedly, with out-of-sample explained variance rising from $0.09$ at June to $0.298$ for ElasticNet and $0.301$ for LSTM. At harvest closure, integrated models reach $R^2_{\text{OOS}} = 0.350$ at county level and an absolute correlation of $r = 0.946$ on macro-regional portfolios. Diebold--Mariano tests confirm that weather features add statistically significant accuracy gains over the secular trend baseline from June onward ($p = 0.0011$).

\subsection{How Does Model Complexity Compare Across Temporal Resolutions?}
The comparative performance of machine learning architectures depends directly on temporal sequence representation. On monthly tabular inputs, regularized linear regression (ElasticNet, $R^2_{\text{OOS}} = 0.320$) remains competitive with non-linear tree ensembles, outperforming Random Forest ($R^2 = 0.252$) and XGBoost ($R^2 = 0.273$). When bioclimatic indicators explicitly embed physiological thresholds ($\text{EDD}_{30}$, $VPD$), linear estimators extract available signals without sample fragmentation, whereas unconstrained tree algorithms exhibit conservative variance attenuation. 

The structural advantage of deep learning (LSTM) lies in its capacity to process high-frequency sequence tensors without parameter proliferation. While linear models are constrained to monthly tabular aggregations to prevent severe dimensionality expansion ($P = 206$ features at $H=1$), recurrent neural networks employ temporal weight sharing across 10-day decad steps ($T=36$). This sub-monthly resolution captures acute short-duration heat spikes and moisture deficits that monthly averages dilute. Consequently, the sequential LSTM achieves superior predictive accuracy ($R^2_{\text{OOS}} = 0.336$), and the linear-recurrent ensemble achieves the highest overall skill ($R^2_{\text{OOS}} = 0.350$, $\text{RMSE} = 4.91\text{ bu/ac}$).

\subsection{Can Empirical Models Anticipate Downside Tail Risk?}
The framework reliably anticipates the direction and relative severity of acute historical shortfall shocks before harvest. At the macro-regional portfolio level, the 2012 historic drought is predicted with high fidelity ($43.53\text{ bu/ac}$ predicted vs $43.55\text{ bu/ac}$ observed). At the county decile level, however, point predictions in the bottom decile ($D_1$) exhibit substantial attenuation (predicting $-2.08\text{ bu/ac}$ against an observed mean shortfall of $-8.82\text{ bu/ac}$). As demonstrated econometrically in Appendix~\ref{chap:appendix_detrending}, this attenuation is an intrinsic mathematical consequence of $L_2$ conditional shrinkage ($\mathbb{E}[Y \mid X]$ under ~70\% unobserved non-climatic noise) rather than detrending lag, persisting identically under an oracle full-sample cubic trend (residual $+8.38\text{ bu/ac}$ vs $+6.74\text{ bu/ac}$ under baseline).

\subsection{Does Prior-Year Yield Drive Forecast Skill?}
Our controlled ablation resolves the apparent contradiction in recent deep learning literature, where autoregressive models attribute over half of predictive accuracy to lagged yield \citep{ChenZhang2026}. In multi-decadal series, raw yield $y_{t-1}$ correlates above $0.80$ with $y_t$ primarily due to secular technological progress. When prior-year yield is detrended strictly on training data ($\epsilon_{t-1} = y_{t-1} - \hat{\tau}_{t-1}$), the net contribution of genuine agronomic carryover (soil moisture memory, rotational benefits) is modest, adding $+1.0\%$ to $+2.0\%$ in $R^2_{\text{OOS}}$ (Table~\ref{tab:raw_vs_anomaly_performance}). Reconstructed raw yield levels reach $R^2_{\text{level}} = 0.651$, demonstrating that high absolute yield fit reflects deterministic secular technology rather than inter-annual climate sensitivity.

\subsection{How Does Accuracy Scale from Counties to Regional Portfolios?}
County-level estimation ($N = 10{,}125$) is essential for econometric identification, providing sufficient sample depth to calibrate non-linear stress thresholds without sample starvation. For commercial supply-chain operations, however, decision-making focuses on the macro-regional supply portfolio. As forecasts aggregate across the 135 study counties, idiosyncratic field-level noise cancels via the law of large numbers ($\sigma / \sqrt{N}$). Across lead times, regional portfolio RMSE declines from $3.84\text{ bu/ac}$ during pre-season ($H=12 \dots 6$, matching the naive baseline) to $2.94\text{ bu/ac}$ at June ($H=4$), $2.77\text{ bu/ac}$ at July ($H=3$), $2.50\text{ bu/ac}$ at August ($H=2$), and $2.16\text{ bu/ac}$ at harvest closure ($H=1^*$). Regional explained variance rises from $0.31$ to $0.71$, with a correlation of $r = 0.946$ with realized regional production (Table~\ref{tab:yearly_macro_yields}).

\section{Confrontation with Existing Literature}
\label{sec:disc_literature}

\subsection{Target Formulation and the Detrending Confound}
Target definitions determine whether published forecast skill reflects genuine weather predictability or spurious correlation with secular technology trends. \citet{ChenZhang2026} report raw-yield $R^2$ values near $0.67$ with lagged yield, but omitting that input reduces score to approximately $0.33$. Our controlled ablation demonstrates that including un-detrended prior yield leaks secular technological drift into the predictor matrix. When the target and lagged yield are strictly detrended on training folds alone, the genuine agronomic carryover contribution is $+1.0\%$ to $+2.0\%$ in $R^2$. Evaluating detrended anomalies aligns with earlier econometric benchmarks \citep{Hoffman2020}, isolating climate vulnerability from trend confounding.

\subsection{Spatial Aggregation: County Disaggregation versus Regional Portfolios}
Studies reporting state or national forecasting accuracy document $R^2$ values above $0.75$ and RMSE below $3.0\text{ bu/ac}$. As demonstrated in Section~\ref{sec:res_macro_portfolio}, our ensemble forecasts achieve a regional RMSE of $2.16\text{ bu/ac}$ and a correlation of $0.946$ when aggregated across the 135-county domain, compared to $4.91\text{ bu/ac}$ at county level. County-level training provides the degrees of freedom required for econometric identification, while spatial portfolio aggregation cancels independent local errors. Comparative evaluations must therefore align observational perimeters: county-level models evaluate local agronomic dispersion, whereas spatial aggregates assess macro-supply volume.

\subsection{Task Formulation: Continuous Regression versus Event Classification}
A separate branch of agricultural forecasting literature frames yield prediction as discrete regime classification or directional shortfall detection \citep{Iizumietal2021, Vijverberg2023}. While categorical models simplify evaluation into hit rates or receiver operating characteristic curves, thresholding continuous yield distributions creates boundary arbitrariness and discards volumetric departure information. Our continuous anomaly regression framework retains quantitative shock magnitude across all $2{,}025$ test county-years, allowing supply-chain operators to calculate physical supply deficits directly.

\subsection{Observational Perimeters: Reanalysis versus Satellite Remote Sensing and Breeding Trials}
Remote-sensing studies incorporate optical and multispectral vegetation indices to track canopy development \citep{Xie2025, Yinetal2026}. While satellite reflectance (NDVI, NIRv) and Solar-Induced Chlorophyll Fluorescence (SIF) measure physiological plant response, their inclusion restricts evaluation to post-2000 records and introduces cloud-cover missingness. By restricting inputs strictly to gridded ERA5-Land reanalysis, our framework evaluates pure atmospheric forzings over a 75-year unbroken historical baseline. Reanalysis variables ($VPD$, $ET_0$, $EDD_{30}$) measure evaporative demand and thermal stress directly, establishing the theoretical limit of weather-only predictability. Controlled breeding trials with genomic features achieve higher accuracy on experimental plots \citep{Shooketal2021}, but evaluate controlled environments distinct from commercial rainfed production.

\subsection{Model Complexity, Inductive Bias, and Temporal Sequences}
Consistent with the systematic review of \citet{Sharmaetal2025}, our tabular results show that gradient-boosted trees and random forests do not outperform regularized linear models on monthly data. When feature engineering explicitly incorporates agronomic stress thresholds, linear hyperplanes effectively partition anomaly space. The advantage of deep learning emerges when sequence architectures process sub-monthly temporal dynamics: recurrent LSTM networks share parameters across 10-day decad steps, resolving intra-monthly heat spikes and moisture deficits that monthly averages dilute. The $1.6$ percentage point advantage of the integrated LSTM over ElasticNet demonstrates that temporal resolution, rather than unconstrained parameter depth, provides the meaningful inductive bias in agricultural forecasting.

\paragraph{Detailed Assessment of Research Hypotheses.}
The empirical findings established in Chapter~\ref{chap:empirical_results} provide structured answers to the five research hypotheses:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Hypothesis 1 (Incremental Weather Skill --- Supported):} Conditioning on observed surface reanalysis produces a statistically significant reduction in out-of-sample forecast error relative to deterministic trend baselines, but strictly within the active growing season. From late June ($H=5$) through harvest closure ($H=1$), one-sided Diebold--Mariano tests confirm that weather features reduce squared prediction loss significantly ($p=0.0011$ for ElasticNet, $p=0.00025$ for LSTM). At harvest, the Linear-Recurrent Ensemble reduces county-level RMSE from $6.124$ to $4.831\text{ bu/ac}$ ($+21.1\%$ skill improvement, $R^2_{\text{OOS}} = 0.378$). Conversely, during dormant overwintering months ($H=11 \dots 7$), surface weather contains zero predictive signal ($R^2_{\text{OOS}} \le 0.00$), and unconstrained models overfit on stochastic cold-season noise.
    
    \item \textbf{Hypothesis 2 (Lead-Time Monotonicity --- Not Supported):} While predictive accuracy expands across major phenological transitions (vegetative emergence $\to$ flowering $\to$ pod-fill), strict step-by-step monotonicity ($R^2(H) \le R^2(H-1)$) fails empirically. Skill trajectories exhibit intermediate plateaus between late August ($H=3$) and late September ($H=2$), as biological seed mass is largely finalized. Furthermore, late-season precipitation introduces post-maturity weather noise that induces performance reversals at harvest in northern counties unless harvest closure dates are synchronized to local crop termination ($H^*(s)=2$).
    
    \item \textbf{Hypothesis 3 (Temporal Aggregation Granularity --- Supported under Model Selection):} Model selection on validation folds confirms that 10-day decad sequences achieve lower validation error than coarse 30-day monthly blocks for sequential architectures. Recurrent parameter sharing enables the LSTM to capture intra-monthly heat spikes ($EDD_{30}$) and acute moisture stress without expanding column dimensions. Conversely, for tabular specifications, monthly blocks remain optimal: expanding tabular feature matrices to sub-monthly pentads or decads induces severe multicollinearity and parameter dilution, degrading out-of-sample generalization.
    
    \item \textbf{Hypothesis 4 (Model Complexity, Inductive Bias, and Linear Parsimony --- Supported):} The empirical evidence corroborates the cautionary hypothesis that highly parameterized deep models do not systematically outperform regularized linear models under strict chronological validation. Across monthly tabular models, tree ensembles (Random Forest, XGBoost) provide no predictive advantage over ElasticNet ($R^2_{\text{OOS}} = 0.300$ for XGBoost vs $0.320$ for ElasticNet), while exhibiting training instability and step-function extrapolation ceilings in bumper crop years ($D_{10}$). While the sequential LSTM achieves the highest individual point accuracy ($R^2_{\text{OOS}} = 0.370$), formal Diebold--Mariano testing ($p=0.162$) shows that its performance difference from ElasticNet (+Lag) is not statistically distinguishable from zero under serial correlation controls. When signal-to-noise ratios are low during early vegetative growth ($H=5, 4$), $\ell_1/\ell_2$ parameter shrinkage provides essential variance control \citep{James2023}, whereas unconstrained deep networks overfit on high-dimensional noise. The Linear-Recurrent Ensemble attains the global optimum ($R^2_{\text{OOS}} = 0.378$, $p=0.017$ over ElasticNet), demonstrating that variance shrinkage and recurrent sequence gating provide complementary strengths rather than deep dominance.
    
    \item \textbf{Hypothesis 5 (Tail-Risk and Extreme Shock Anticipation --- Supported with Qualifications):} Continuous regression models successfully anticipate catastrophic macro-regional yield shortfalls months before harvest. The 2012 flash drought is detected by late July ($H=4$) and fully resolved by late August ($H=3$) with near-zero regional error ($43.53$ predicted vs $43.55\text{ bu/ac}$ observed). However, at the local county decile scale ($D_1$), continuous models exhibit substantial conditional shrinkage toward the historical mean (realized mean shortfall $-8.82\text{ bu/ac}$ vs predicted $-2.18\text{ bu/ac}$). This attenuation is an inherent mathematical property of $L_2$ loss minimization under partial observational noise rather than a structural modeling defect.
\end{enumerate}

A critical methodological check concerns whether these verdicts depend on baseline detrending or potential technological structural breaks. In Appendix~\ref{chap:appendix_detrending}, we evaluate a sequential Chow test across the 75-year panel and stress-test the entire forecasting framework under an oracle full-sample cubic trend. The Chow test confirms that during the pre-test period (1951--2010), trend stability cannot be rejected ($F_{\max} = 2.85 < F_{\text{crit}} = 4.01$, $p=0.093$), justifying the causal linear baseline ex-ante. A post-hoc level shift of $+4.83\text{ bu/ac}$ emerges in 2014 ($p < 0.0001$). Under the oracle cubic trend, all substantive conclusions hold: model rankings are unchanged, skill emerges in August, and decile $D_1$ under-prediction persists identically (residual $+8.38\text{ bu/ac}$ vs $+6.74\text{ bu/ac}$ under baseline). This confirms that tail attenuation is an inherent consequence of $L_2$ loss shrinkage under imperfectly observed weather inputs, rather than an artifact of detrending lag.

\section{Operational Implications for Agrifood Supply Chains}
\label{sec:disc_operational}

The empirical findings generate practical guidance for agribusiness risk management, commodity merchandising, and logistical planning:

\subsection{Lead-Time Value and Decision Timelines}
Commercial supply-chain decisions operate across distinct planning horizons. Fertilizer procurement, seed purchasing, and initial acreage allocations occur 6 to 9 months before harvest ($H=10 \dots 7$). The lack of pre-sowing predictive skill indicates that atmospheric weather reanalysis cannot inform these early capital commitments. Procurement managers should rely on baseline trend projections during winter and early spring rather than trading speculative weather anomalies. Operational utility concentrates between late June ($H=5$) and late August ($H=3$). In late July, the emergence of the weather signal allows grain handlers to adjust river barge commitments, elevator storage capacity, and export hedging. The 2012 drought illustrates this operational value: our ensemble forecast projected a major regional yield collapse by the end of July ($H=4$), months before USDA NASS finalized production estimates in November.

\subsection{Asymmetric Loss Functions in Commodity Trading}
Standard machine learning models trained under mean squared error ($L_2$) treat over-prediction and under-prediction symmetrically. In physical agrifood supply chains, this cost structure is highly asymmetric. Failing to anticipate a severe crop shortfall ($D_1$) results in unfulfilled export contracts, vessel demurrage penalties, and costly spot-market substitution. In contrast, over-predicting yield during a bumper season creates manageable surplus storage costs. Because quadratic loss shrinks predictions toward the mean, risk managers must incorporate quantile-adjusted or asymmetric loss adjustments when translating model outputs into procurement thresholds.

\section{Methodological Boundaries and Limitations}
\label{sec:disc_limitations}

Four boundary conditions govern the interpretation and generalizability of our findings:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Reanalysis Latency versus Real-Time Operations:} ERA5-Land reanalysis is released with an operational latency of 2 to 3 months. Our retrospective benchmark delineates the theoretical upper bound of predictability from consolidated reanalysis fields. Operational deployment requires pairing the framework with near-real-time preliminary streams (such as ECMWF ERA5T with a 5-day latency) or operational numerical weather prediction analyses.
    \item \textbf{Panel Selection and Survivorship:} The 75-year panel balances 135 counties continuously from 1951 to 2025 without imputation. This design prioritizes multidecadal temporal depth to sample rare historical droughts, but introduces geographic selection toward high-productivity commercial counties. Findings represent commercial rainfed production in the Midwestern Corn Belt, and cannot be extrapolated directly to marginal or arid fringes.
    \item \textbf{Unobserved Agronomic Drivers:} Drainage tile networks, supplemental irrigation, localized planting dates, and hybrid maturity selections are not observed as predictors. Residual spatial autocorrelation in model errors reflects these omitted physical mechanisms.
    \item \textbf{Harvest-Timing Calendar Contamination:} Across northern study fringes such as Minnesota, soybean harvesting concludes in early October. Applying the phenological harvest closure rule $H^*(s)$ (September 30 for Minnesota, October 31 elsewhere) resolves post-harvest contamination. Fixed calendar dates in unadjusted specifications inject uninformative late-season weather into models.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Methodological Recommendations and Future Research}
\label{sec:disc_conclusions}

\subsection{Methodological Recommendations}
The empirical findings establish four methodological principles for agricultural forecasting:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Mandatory Train-Only Detrending:} Autoregressive models ingesting un-detrended historical yields leak secular technological progress into predictor matrices, inflating out-of-sample scores through trend persistence rather than weather sensitivity. All detrending models must be estimated strictly on training folds.
    \item \textbf{Chronological Expanding-Window Validation:} Random cross-validation splits over county-years induce severe spatial and temporal leakage. Reliable validation requires strictly chronological expanding windows that respect operational decision constraints.
    \item \textbf{Obligatory Parsimonious Baselines:} Regularized linear models and naive secular trends must serve as mandatory baselines. Claims of machine learning or deep learning superiority are invalid unless benchmarked against properly regularized linear specifications.
    \item \textbf{Dual Spatial Reporting:} Forecasting architectures should report performance at both county level (to evaluate local identification and extreme-tail shrinkage) and regional portfolio scale (to assess macro-supply volume).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Avenues for Future Research}
Several productive research trajectories emerge from the boundaries of this work:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Coupling with Dynamical Climate Forecasts:} Integrating 10-to-15-day numerical weather prediction ensembles at active horizons ($H=4, 3$) and seasonal dynamical climate projections (such as ECMWF SEAS5) at pre-sowing horizons ($H \ge 6$) could extend actionable lead times into spring.
    \item \textbf{Dynamic Phenological Indexing:} Replacing fixed solar calendar months with phenological coordinates anchored to estimated county sowing dates (Days After Sowing, DAS) would normalize planting delays caused by wet spring conditions.
    \item \textbf{Phenology-Guided Multimodal Satellite Fusion:} Fusing high-resolution optical reflectance (NIRv) and chlorophyll fluorescence (SIF) strictly during reproductive pod-filling (July--August) would calibrate canopy physiological stress without truncating multidecadal historical baselines.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Crop Cross-Commodity Architectures:} Expanding the expanding-window framework jointly to corn and soybeans would test whether shared agro-climatic representations enhance early-season predictability across rotational cropping systems.
    \item \textbf{Spatio-Temporal Graph Neural Networks:} Explicitly encoding county topological adjacency, soil drainage characteristics, and watershed boundaries into graph convolution architectures (ST-GCN) would capture spatial shock propagation.
\end{enumerate}
